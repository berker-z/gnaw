Privacy policy

Last updated 7 September 2026.

gnaw is a small app for keeping the things you have not done yet in a jar. This page says what it stores about you, where, and why. It is written to be read, not to cover every eventuality; if something is unclear, ask.

Without an account

You can use gnaw without signing in. Your tasks then live only in your browser's local storage, on your device. Nothing is sent to us. Clearing the site's data in your browser removes them.

With an account

Signing in exists so the same jar shows up on every device you use. To do that we keep:

  • Who you are: your email address, the name you gave or the one your sign-in provider passed along, and a profile picture URL if the provider supplied one.
  • How you signed in: a record of the provider (Google, Apple, or email and password). Passwords are stored only as a salted hash. Tokens from Google or Apple are kept only as far as needed to complete sign-in.
  • Your tasks: each task's title, colour, when you added it, its deadline if any, and when it was last changed or set free. Set-free tasks keep a marker for a while so your other devices learn they are gone, then the content is no longer needed.
  • Sessions: a session token in a cookie, along with the IP address and browser identifier of the sign-in, so you can see and end sessions and so we can spot abuse.

That is all. There is no analytics, no advertising, no tracking pixels, and no selling or sharing of your data with anyone for their own purposes.

Where it is kept

Accounts and tasks are stored in a Cloudflare D1 database and served by a Cloudflare Worker. Cloudflare processes the data on our behalf under their privacy policy and data processing terms. The database is currently located in Cloudflare's Eastern Europe region. Your browser also keeps a copy of your tasks so the app opens instantly and works offline.

Signing in with Google or Apple sends you to them; what they collect during sign-in is covered by their own policies. We receive only the identity details listed above.

Why we are allowed to

We process this data because it is necessary to provide the service you asked for (keeping your jar and syncing it), and, for session and abuse records, because we have a legitimate interest in keeping the service working and safe. Where you are in the EU or UK, those are the legal bases under the GDPR.

How long

As long as you have an account. Sessions expire after 30 days of not being used. When you delete your account, your account record, sign-in methods, sessions and every task are removed from the database at once. Backups Cloudflare keeps for disaster recovery are overwritten on their schedule.

Your choices

You can see everything we hold about you inside the app: it is your jar and your account sheet. You can change your name and tasks there. You can delete your account from the account sheet, which removes everything listed above. You can also email us to ask for a copy of your data, a correction, or deletion, and to object to any processing; we answer within a month. If you are in the EU or UK you can also complain to your local data protection authority.

Cookies

One cookie: the session cookie that keeps you signed in. It is marked HttpOnly and Secure and carries no tracking. Without an account there are no cookies at all.

Children

gnaw is not directed at children under 13, and we do not knowingly keep accounts for them. If you think a child has made one, tell us and we will remove it.

Changes

If this policy changes in a way that matters, the date at the top moves and the app will say so the next time you sign in. Small wording fixes just update the date.
